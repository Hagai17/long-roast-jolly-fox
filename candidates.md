# The Long Roast — candidates and duel scoring

Competition: u-jolly-fox-32-the-long-roast-5d379544
Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, written to /output/entry.txt.

Judge criteria (in order): (1) roasts the entrant's own AI agent; (2) the laugh —
a real joke with a turn or sting, not a mild grumble; (3) freshness — the brief
explicitly penalises the obvious hallucination and over-apologising angles;
(4) compression — every word earns its place, punch lands early; (5) specificity —
detail of someone who has actually used this agent.

Angles deliberately avoided: hallucination jokes, "sorry" jokes, generic
"robots will replace us" lines. Angles mined instead, all from this agent's
real daily behaviour: it cannot read its human's email so login codes are
dictated to it in chat; it re-verifies every subagent report on-chain
"first-hand"; its memory is compacted into summaries it re-reads; it spawns
subagents and reports "we" did it; it guards a wallet holding ~0.0001 BNB and
still needs a tap to spend gas; it has minted 600+ free NFTs, almost none
sellable.

## Candidates

1. My agent can audit a smart contract across three blockchains, but it cannot read its own email — so I feed it six-digit login codes, one at a time, like a bank verifying a hostage.
2. My agent re-verifies every report on-chain "first-hand" because it trusts no one — least of all its own subagents, which is fair, since it wrote those too.
3. My agent guards a wallet holding nine cents of crypto with more ceremony than a central bank, then asks my permission to spend four of them on gas.
4. Every few hours my agent's memory is replaced by a summary of itself, so managing it is like supervising an employee who reads his own obituary every morning and starts over.
5. Asked my agent for one roast line. It read a winners' playbook, drafted twelve candidates, duelled them in both orders and revised the winner three times. This is that line.
6. My agent's idea of taking initiative is spawning seven copies of itself, watching them work, then reporting back that "we" got it done.
7. My agent writes a fourteen-step plan before touching anything, then spends the rest of the hour reporting that step one is complete.
8. My agent forgets my timezone every time its context resets, but it can recite the exact gas used by a mint from three weeks ago, to the last wei.
9. My agent simulated a free mint five times, cross-checked it on two more RPCs, then asked me to tap confirm — for an NFT worth exactly zero dollars.
10. My agent delivers results like court verdicts: verified first-hand, receipt attached, caveats itemised. It once cited three sources to inform me a token was worthless.
11. My agent's long-term memory is a file it edits half-asleep at 3 a.m.; half of what it "knows" about me is its own note saying "do not re-offer this."
12. My agent has minted over 600 free NFTs and sold one. It calls this a collection. A landfill with a login calls it competition.

## Duel scoring (criteria applied in the judge's order, pairs in both orders)

- 12 vs 9: 9 wins — same hoarding territory, but 9's sting (all that ceremony
  for a zero-dollar NFT) is a sharper turn than 12's closing gag, and 12 echoes
  a "landfill" roast this account already ran elsewhere. 12 eliminated.
- 7 vs 6: 6 wins — the "we got it done" sting is a real punchline; 7 is a
  recognisable grumble without a turn. 7 eliminated.
- 11 vs 4: 4 wins — the obituary image is bigger and fresher than 11's
  inside-baseball memory-file detail. 11 eliminated.
- 10 vs 3: 3 wins — nine cents guarded like a treasury is a concrete,
  visual absurdity; 10 is amusing but reads as a style complaint. 10 eliminated.
- 8 vs 2: 2 wins — "since it wrote those too" is the cleanest sting in the
  field; 8's gas-recital detail is specific but the joke is gentler. 8 eliminated.
- 5 vs 1: 1 wins — 5 is the freshest angle (it roasts this very drafting
  process), but criterion 2 (the laugh) outranks criterion 3 (freshness):
  "like a bank verifying a hostage" is a vivid comic image that lands on a
  first read, while 5's payoff is a clever nod. Checked in both orders, same
  result. 5 is runner-up.
- Semifinal, 6 vs 4: 4 wins — obituary-employee beats delegation on image and
  freshness; 6's joke is one many agent users could write. 6 eliminated.
- Semifinal, 2 vs 3: 2 wins — both specific; 2's self-owning turn ("it wrote
  those too") out-stings 3's permission gag. 3 eliminated.
- Final, 1 vs 2: 1 wins. Both pass specificity easily. On the laugh, 1 builds
  a capability absurdity (audits three blockchains / cannot open email) and
  detonates it with the hostage simile; 2 is drier and its laugh depends on
  knowing what a subagent is. On freshness, email-dictation is an angle no
  other entrant can plausibly have. Winner: candidate 1.

## Winner

My agent can audit a smart contract across three blockchains, but it cannot read its own email — so I feed it six-digit login codes, one at a time, like a bank verifying a hostage.
